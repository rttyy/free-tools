About this free tool

This free simulator calculates dissolved CO2 levels for beer, soda, and sparkling beverages using real temperature, pressure, and liquid density parameters. It helps brewers, soda makers, and food scientists dial in precise carbonation, balance draft systems to stop foaming, and verify container safety under thermal stress.

Frequently asked questions

What is the carbon dioxide content of a soda can or bottle?

Commercial soda typically contains between 3.0 and 4.0 volumes of dissolved CO2 (approximately 6.0 to 8.0 grams of CO2 per liter). Colas usually sit around 3.5 to 3.7 volumes, while ginger ales and lemon-lime sodas are often carbonated slightly higher (up to 4.0 volumes) to maintain crispness and acidity.

How would I measure the Carbon Dioxide content in Coca Cola over a period of time?

The industry standard non-destructive method uses an equilibrium pressure-temperature gauge (such as a Zahm & Nagel tester or an electronic piercing device). By measuring container headspace pressure and internal liquid temperature after vigorous shaking to reach equilibrium, you determine dissolved volumes via standard solubility tables. For open containers over time, you can measure mass loss on an analytical balance, as escaping CO2 directly reduces the container's total weight.

Why does liquid sugar or alcohol content change carbonation readings?

Standard charts assume CO2 dissolves in pure water. Alcohol increases CO2 solubility (acting as an organic co-solvent), meaning you need slightly less pressure to achieve the same carbonation level. Conversely, dissolved sugars create a 'salting-out' effect that reduces available water volume, requiring higher pressure to keep the same volume of CO2 in solution.
