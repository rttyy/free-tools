This free simulator helps homesteaders, market gardeners, and greenhouse owners calculate conductive heat loss, air infiltration, and peak heating demands. Compare glazing materials side-by-side, model water barrel thermal mass, and forecast fuel expenses across varying winter conditions.

How to use it

  1. Enter your greenhouse dimensions (length, width, wall height, and roof pitch) to determine total glazing surface area.

Frequently asked questions

How to convert inches to gallons?

To find gallons from dimensions measured in inches, first calculate the container volume in cubic inches (Length × Width × Height for rectangular containers, or π × Radius² × Height for round drums). Then, divide the total cubic inches by 231, because one US liquid gallon equals exactly 231 cubic inches.

Glass vs plastic greenhouse covering: pros and cons

Glass offers high clarity, long lifespan, and resists UV degradation, but it has poor insulation values (U-value ~1.1–1.2 for single pane), heavy weight, and higher framing costs. Multi-wall plastic (polycarbonate) provides significantly better thermal retention (U-value ~0.58–0.65 for 8mm twin-wall), high impact resistance, and easier installation, but it gradually loses light transmittance over 10–15 years due to UV exposure.

How does draft infiltration affect total greenhouse heating requirements?

Infiltration accounts for cold outdoor air leaking through frame seams, vents, and foundation gaps. In drafty or unsealed hobby structures, air leakage can add 20% to 40% on top of conductive glazing losses, requiring a substantially larger heater output to maintain target temperatures.
