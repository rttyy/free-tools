About this free tool

This interactive tool calculates precise iron acetate formulations, supplemental tannin dosing, and neutralization washes for wood finishing. Designed for woodworkers and luthiers, it lets you simulate grain contrast and darkness across various wood species before applying reactive chemical finishes to raw lumber.

How to use it

  1. Select your lumber species to load its natural baseline tannin profile.
  2. Input batch volume to calculate required supplemental tannin powder and hot water dosing.
  3. Set vinegar concentration and steel wool mass to calculate steep time and residual acidity.
  4. Review the simulated grain contrast preview and print your bench formulation sheet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the chemical reaction between iron acetate and wood tannins?

Steel wool reacts with acetic acid in vinegar to form iron(II) acetate. When applied to wood, the dissolved iron bonds with polyphenolic compounds (tannins) inside the wood cell structure, oxidizing into ferric tannate, an insoluble organometallic pigment that dyes the wood grain pitch black.

Why did my iron acetate turn wood gray or purple instead of deep black?

Species like pine, maple, poplar, and birch naturally contain very few tannins. Without sufficient polyphenols to bond with the iron, the reaction yields a weak gray or blotchy purple. Applying a pre-wash of dissolved tannin powder (such as quebracho or chestnut) restores the stoichiometric balance needed for deep black.

How does residual iron acetate acid damage finishes, and how do I prevent it?

Incompletely reacted iron solutions contain leftover acetic acid that remains in the wood grain. This acidity inhibits drying oils like linseed or tung oil from polymerizing and can degrade shellac or lacquer. Wiping the thoroughly dried piece with a mild sodium bicarbonate (baking soda) wash neutralizes residual acid.

How do I know when an iron acetate batch is fully reacted?

The steep is ready when hydrogen gas bubbles stop forming, the liquid turns amber-gray, and the steel wool has largely dissolved. Using a slight excess of steel wool ensures all acetic acid is consumed. Always filter through paper coffee filters before bench application to remove settled iron sludge.
