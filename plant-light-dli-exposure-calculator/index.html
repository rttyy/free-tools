About this free tool

This tool calculates exact Daily Light Integral (DLI) and PPFD from Lux, Foot-candles, or grow light specs. It helps indoor gardeners, citrus growers, and houseplant owners identify winter light deficits and optimize light heights. Use the interactive web version for free or download the standalone offline HTML pack.

How to use it

  1. Select your light source type (Sunlight, White LED, Blurple LED, HPS, or custom spectrum).
  2. Enter your light meter reading in Lux or Foot-candles, or input PPFD directly.
  3. Set your daily photoperiod duration in hours.
  4. View calculated DLI and compare it against target light requirements for your plant species.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use PLA 3D-printed parts outdoors for plant setups?

Standard PLA plastic degrades under prolonged outdoor UV exposure and deforms at temperatures around 55°C (131°F) in direct sunlight. For outdoor garden stakes, pots, or light mounts, use PETG or ASA plastic instead, as they offer significantly higher UV and thermal resistance.

How important is full sun for a Blue Spruce tree?

Blue Spruce (Picea pungens) requires full outdoor direct sunlight—at least 6 to 8 hours daily, corresponding to a DLI of 30+ mol/m²/day. They are outdoor cold-climate conifers and will rapidly decline, drop needles, and die if kept indoors or in shady conditions.

Am I meeting the light requirements for indoor dwarf citrus trees?

Dwarf citrus trees require high light levels, ideally 15 to 25 DLI (mol/m²/day). Indoor window sunlight during autumn and winter rarely delivers more than 3 to 6 DLI. If your tree is shedding green leaves, you likely need high-output LED supplemental lighting running 12 to 16 hours daily.

Why are my Tillandsia (air plants) turning black and dying after watering?

Black, soft bases indicate rot caused by water trapped between the leaf bases in poor airflow or low-light conditions. After soaking or misting Tillandsia, shake off excess water gently and place them upside down in a well-ventilated area with bright indirect light (DLI 4–8) so they dry completely within 4 hours.
