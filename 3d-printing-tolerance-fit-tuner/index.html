About this free tool

The 3D Printing Precision Fit & Tolerance Tuner helps hobbyists and mechanical designers produce parts that fit together cleanly on the first print. By analyzing caliper measurements from a simple test object, the tool calculates exact CAD offsets for press, slip, and loose mechanical fits. The core calculator runs free in your browser, with an offline pack available for workshop use.

How to use it

  1. Print a basic tolerance test coupon using your target printer, filament, and slicer settings.
  2. Measure the physical printed hole and pin dimensions using digital calipers.
  3. Input your target nominal size and physical measurements into the tuner calculator.
  4. Apply the generated target CAD dimensions directly to your 3D modeling software.

Frequently asked questions

What tool can I use to measure dimensions in STL files?

STL files are triangular mesh models rather than parametric solids, making native measurement tricky. You can measure distances in STL files using free tools like PrusaSlicer (built-in measuring tool), MeshLab, or Blender. Once you know your intended STL dimensions, our tuner calculates the real-world clearance offsets needed for printing.

What is the best method for producing closely fitting 3D printed parts?

Producing closely fitting parts requires separating printer axis calibration from material shrinkage and slicer wall expansion. Print a test block, measure the inner hole and outer peg variances with digital calipers, and apply clearance offsets (0.05mm for press fit, 0.15mm for slip fit) directly to your CAD sketch dimensions rather than scaling the entire model.

Which resolution is needed for bricks compatible with Lego?

Lego-compatible bricks require high horizontal dimensional accuracy rather than micro layer heights; a standard 0.12mm to 0.16mm layer height with a 0.4mm nozzle works great. The key is controlling wall expansion: studs typically require an exact 0.05mm to 0.08mm side clearance so the friction snap ('clutch power') works without stress-cracking the ABS or PLA.
