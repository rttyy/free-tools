About this free tool

The Visual Depth of Field & Perspective Lab is an interactive optical geometry simulator designed for photographers, educators, and visual artists. It allows you to visualize optical depth, blur falloff, and lens perspective in real time rather than memorizing abstract math equations. Try the free web version directly in your browser, or download the full offline HTML pack for fieldwork.

How to use it

  1. Select your camera sensor size, focal length, and aperture settings on the control panel.
  2. Adjust the subject distance slider to observe real-time changes in depth of field and background blur falloff.
  3. Enable Dual Mode to compare two different camera setups or lenses side-by-side.
  4. Input your target venue dimensions to calculate the exact focal length required for specific framing limits.

Frequently asked questions

Which lens serves best as the only lens on a cropped body for a beginner: the 40mm f2.8 or 50mm f1.8?

For a single lens on an APS-C crop sensor body, the 40mm f/2.8 pancake lens offers a more versatile natural field of view (~60-64mm full-frame equivalent) compared to the tighter perspective of a 50mm (~75-80mm equivalent). While the 50mm f/1.8 creates a shallower depth of field at wider apertures, the 40mm is generally easier to use in tighter indoor spaces and daily walkaround situations.

Is there a 'rule of thumb' that I can use to estimate depth of field while shooting?

A classic field rule of thumb is that depth of field extends approximately 1/3 in front of your focal point and 2/3 behind it at medium subject distances. However, for quick calculations, remember that doubling your working distance quadruples your depth of field, while stopping down by 2 full stops doubles it.

Estimating focal length range required for shooting scenario

You can estimate the required focal length using the formula: Focal Length = (Distance to Subject × Sensor Dimension) / Field of View Size. For example, to fit a 2-meter tall subject from 10 meters away on a full-frame sensor (24mm vertical height), you need a focal length of (10m × 24mm) / 2m = 120mm.

How to estimate pixels per degree for an image from a camera phone using EXIF data?

Extract the image width in pixels, physical lens focal length, and sensor width from the file's EXIF data. First, compute the horizontal Angular Field of View using: FOV = 2 × arctan(Sensor Width / (2 × Focal Length)) converted to degrees. Then divide the image pixel width by the calculated FOV degrees to get pixels per degree.
