    About this free tool

    This free calculator helps homeowners and landscape contractors translate in-ground excavation dimensions into true loose volume, wet dirt weight, and wheelbarrow trips. It accounts for soil expansion and weight variations so you can rent the right dumpster, trailer, or equipment without paying overage penalties.

    How to use it

    1. Enter the length, width, and excavation depth of your project area.
    2. Select your soil type (topsoil, clay, sandy loam) and moisture condition to calculate loose swell and weight.
    3. Review the total loose cubic yards, estimated wet tonnage, and required wheelbarrow trips.
    4. Input your trailer payload or dumpster weight limit to verify haul safety before digging.

    Frequently asked questions

    What's the best way to remove 2-6 inches of dirt from my yard?

    For areas under 150 square feet, use a flat transfer shovel and square-point spade to cut the perimeter, peel up sod sections, and scrape the soil down to depth. For areas between 150 and 800 square feet, rent a motorized sod cutter set to its maximum depth to strip the organic layer, followed by manual shoveling or a power wheelbarrow. For spaces over 800 square feet or heavy clay, renting a mini skid-steer with a smooth bucket or a mini excavator saves dozens of hours of manual labor.

    What's the best way to remove grass/topsoil to prepare for grading?

    The cleanest method is using a walk-behind sod cutter rather than a rototiller. A tiller churns living roots, weed seeds, and soil together, creating uneven, spongy ground that will settle unpredictably later. A sod cutter shears root networks at a uniform depth (typically 1.5 to 2.5 inches), allowing you to roll up the sod and expose a flat, firm subgrade ready for fill, gravel, or finished grading.

    What is the appropriate spacing for 3' x 5' bluestone pavers?

    For dry-laid bluestone on a compacted aggregate and sand or stone dust base, leave a consistent 3/16-inch to 3/8-inch joint gap if the stone has sawn thermal edges. If the pavers have irregular, natural-cleft, or hand-chiseled edges, maintain a wider 1/2-inch to 3/4-inch joint to absorb edge variations. For stepping stone layouts surrounded by lawn or groundcover, place them roughly 24 to 26 inches apart on-center to match a natural walking stride.

    Why is my excavated dirt pile so much bigger than the hole I dug?

    In-ground soil has settled and compacted naturally over decades. When you dig it out, you introduce air pockets between the grains, a phenomenon known as soil swell. Topsoil typically swells by 15% to 25%, while dense clay can expand by up to 35% or more in loose volume. A hole measuring exactly 2 cubic yards in the ground regularly yields 2.5 to 2.7 cubic yards of loose dirt to haul away.
