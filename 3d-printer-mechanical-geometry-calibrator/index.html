About this free tool

This free browser-based geometry tool helps 3D printer builders and modders eliminate frame skew, bed warping, and mechanical misalignment without spreadsheets. Input digital caliper measurements to generate Marlin M852 correction g-code, compute thermal expansion hole clearances, and convert bed probe deltas into precise leveling screw adjustments.

How to use it

  1. Print an orthogonal calibration square or cross and measure the side lengths and diagonals using digital calipers.
  2. Enter your measurements into the skew calculator to compute angular error and generate Marlin M852 or Klipper values.
  3. Send the generated M852 g-code to your printer terminal and save it to EEPROM with M500.
  4. Use the thermal clearance and tramming tabs to verify mounting hole tolerances and adjust bed leveling thumbwheels.

Frequently asked questions

Anet A8 - X and Y axis not square - how to fix?

On the Anet A8 acrylic frame, skew is almost always caused by uneven spacing along the threaded M8 rods forming the lower H-frame. Loosen the M8 frame nuts and use digital calipers to measure the exact distance between the front and rear acrylic plates on both the left and right sides. Once both distances match perfectly, tighten the locking nuts sequentially against the acrylic. If slight racking persists due to the flexible frame material, you can apply Marlin M852 software skew compensation to correct the remaining deviation in firmware.

How to re-program Prusa firmware to accept a taller Z axis?

To increase Z travel on stock Prusa firmware, you must recompile the Prusa-Firmware source code. Open the source in Arduino IDE or PlatformIO, locate `Configuration_prusa.h`, and edit the `Z_MAX_POS` value to reflect your new physical hardware limit. After compiling and flashing the new hex file, rerun the XYZ calibration wizard; if your custom frame is significantly taller, you may also need to adjust the Z-calibration search limits in `ultralcd.cpp` to prevent timeout errors.

How to calculate linear Y rod length for a larger replacement bed?

The required linear rod length equals your desired bed travel plus the spacing between the outermost linear bearing edges, plus endstop and frame mounting margins. Your travel should match your new bed length plus roughly 10 to 20mm of nozzle over-travel. Add the distance from the front edge of the front Y bearing to the rear edge of the rear Y bearing on your carriage, then add an additional 25 to 30mm for rod ends seated in frame brackets.

How thick does an aluminum print bed need to be?

For small beds up to 235x235mm, a 3mm to 4mm plate is adequate, though cast tooling plate performs significantly better than standard rolled sheet. For beds 300x300mm or larger, choose at least 6mm to 8mm cast aluminum tooling plate (such as MIC6 or ATP-5). Rolled aluminum contains internal mechanical stresses that cause severe center warping under heat cycles, whereas cast tooling plate is stress-relieved and precision-milled flat.
