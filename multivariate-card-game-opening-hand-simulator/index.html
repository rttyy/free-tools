About this free tool

This free tool calculates exact joint hypergeometric opening hand probabilities across multiple card categories for competitive TCG deck builders. Instead of testing one card at a time, you can define joint requirements—like hitting land thresholds alongside specific curve plays. It runs client-side in any modern browser without accounts or tracking.

How to use it

  1. Set your total deck size and opening hand size (e.g., 60 and 7).
  2. Add distinct card categories (such as Lands, 1-Drops, or Combo Pieces) with their respective deck counts.
  3. Specify your target success range for each category (e.g., minimum 2 lands, maximum 4 lands, minimum 1 play).
  4. Review the exact joint success probability, mulligan progression curves, and export results to CSV if needed.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the likelihood of drawing certain cards in your opening hand?

To calculate the probability of drawing specific cards, use the hypergeometric distribution formula: C(k, x) * C(N - k, n - x) / C(N, n), where N is deck size, k is total target cards in deck, n is hand size, and x is the target number drawn. When tracking multiple distinct card groups simultaneously (such as lands and specific spells), you use the multivariate hypergeometric distribution to account for dependent sampling without replacement.

How long will an MTG draft take with varying number of players (approximately)?

A standard 8-player Magic: The Gathering booster draft typically takes between 2.5 and 3.5 hours. Drafting and deck building take roughly 45 to 60 minutes, followed by three 50-minute Swiss rounds. Smaller drafts (4 to 6 players) or casual formats take about 1.5 to 2 hours, while larger pods run longer due to round coordination.

Why do I need a multivariate calculator instead of a standard hypergeometric calculator?

Standard calculators only compute the probability of drawing one specific card type in isolation. In actual gameplay, a keepable hand depends on combinations (for example, 2-4 lands AND at least one castable spell). Because drawing a card from one group removes it from the deck and shifts the remaining odds, calculating joint probabilities requires multivariate formulas to produce mathematically accurate results.
