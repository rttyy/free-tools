About this free tool

Burst Action Sequence Compositor allows photographers, videographers, and athletic coaches to create multi-exposure action sequences directly in their web browser. Using client-side background subtraction, the tool isolates moving subjects from burst frames without manual masking or cloud uploads. It is completely free to use online, with an optional offline edition for field production.

How to use it

  1. Mount your camera on a tripod and capture an action burst using fixed manual exposure and focus.
  2. Drop your sequence frames into the browser interface to automatically calculate the background plate.
  3. Adjust the subtraction threshold and scrub the timeline to select which action frames to include.
  4. Optional: Enable chromatic velocity tinting or inspect trajectory paths across the movement arc.
  5. Export your finished composite at full original resolution or save a reusable session preset.

Frequently asked questions

How do I combine multiple exposures for action shots?

To combine multiple action exposures, capture a sequence on a locked tripod so the background remains identical across all frames. In software, each frame is layered over a base plate, and pixels that differ from the background (the athlete or subject) are isolated using difference masks or automated subtraction. The isolated subjects are then blended onto the final static background frame.

Why do it "in-camera" rather than in post-processing?

In-camera multi-exposure modes are convenient for instant results, but they add exposure values together directly on the sensor, which frequently causes overexposure or washed-out subjects when blending more than two or three frames. Processing in post-processing allows you to use selective masking and background subtraction, ensuring the background remains properly exposed and each instance of the subject retains its full contrast and sharpness.

Do my burst photos need to be shot from a locked tripod?

Yes, automated background subtraction relies on a static background between frames to detect the moving subject. Handheld bursts with camera movement will cause alignment errors, requiring manual frame registration or manual masking. For best results, use a sturdy tripod with fixed focal length, manual focus, and locked manual exposure.
