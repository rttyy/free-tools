About this free tool

The Breath-Synced Mobility & Kinetic Stretch Composer lets desk workers, runners, and fitness enthusiasts design and play mobility routines synchronized to custom breathing cadences. It runs directly in your browser with no account setup or subscriptions required. Use the free online version to balance your nervous system, or grab the offline pack for full JSON exports and printable routine cards.

How to use it

  1. Enter your total available break duration and select your target inhale/exhale breath ratio.
  2. Select your mobility poses to automatically reverse-solve exact breath cycles and duration per stretch.
  3. Compare vagal stimulation scores across different breath ratios to optimize parasympathetic recovery.
  4. Press play to follow the browser-based visual breath pacing guide live, or export printable routine summary cards.

Frequently asked questions

Is the basic version of the composer free to use?

Yes, the core web version is completely free and runs directly in your web browser without requiring account registration. You can design, calculate, and play breath-guided mobility flows immediately. The paid $9 Gumroad product provides an offline pack with full JSON export capabilities, scenario saving, and printable summary cards.

How does the tool calculate pose timing based on breathing cadences?

The composer uses a reverse-solving algorithm that takes your total available time slot and desired inhale/exhale ratio. It then determines the exact number of full breath cycles per pose so your routine finishes precisely on time. This ensures you never have to check a clock during your break or warmup.

Can I use this tool offline without an internet connection?

The paid $9 offline pack includes self-contained files that open in any modern web browser without needing internet access. You can save your routines locally as JSON files or print physical posture cards for your gym bag or desk. This makes it ideal for travel, remote workouts, or minimal digital distraction.

How does breath-synced stretching help with vagal stimulation and relaxation?

Extending your exhale relative to your inhale (such as a 4-second inhale with a 6-second exhale) signals the vagus nerve to slow your heart rate. By locking kinetic stretch transitions to these specific ratios, the composer encourages a parasympathetic state, reducing muscle tension caused by prolonged sitting or high-intensity training.
