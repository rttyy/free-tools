About this free tool

The 2D Orbital Transfer & Gravity Mission Planner is a free browser-based sandbox that lets students, educators, and game developers plan orbital burns and calculate Hohmann transfers visually. It displays real-time trajectory projections, delta-v budgets, and telemetry readouts like eccentricity and orbital period. An offline single-file HTML pack is also available for distraction-free use without accounts or internet access.

How to use it

  1. Select your central body gravitational parameter and set initial orbit parameters (radius and velocity).
  2. Set your target orbit altitude to instantly generate Hohmann transfer delta-v requirements and burn timings.
  3. Apply prograde or retrograde burns manually to observe real-time trajectory overlays and eccentricity shifts.
  4. Export telemetry data to JSON or CSV for physics assignments or game engine integration.

Frequently asked questions

What is the formula to predict lunar and solar eclipses accurately?

Eclipse prediction cannot be solved with a single algebraic equation because it requires calculating when the Moon's phase (syzygy) coincides with its crossing of the ecliptic plane (nodes). Astronomers use the Saros cycle (roughly 6,585.3 days) for cyclical recurrence, paired with Besselian elements and numerical ephemerides (like JPL DE440) to solve the precise geometry of the Sun-Earth-Moon shadow cones over time.

Exercise: 2D orbital mechanics simulation (python)

To build a minimal 2D orbital simulation in Python, model Newton's law of gravitation using numerical integration like Velocity Verlet or Symplectic Euler. At each timestep `dt`, compute distance `r = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)` and gravitational acceleration `a = -G*M / r^2`. Update velocity via `vx += a * (x/r) * dt` and `vy += a * (y/r) * dt`, then update coordinates `x += vx * dt` and `y += vy * dt`.

Is there a upper limit to the number of planets orbiting a star?

Yes, orbital stability imposes a physical limit determined by mutual Hill radii. For planets to avoid chaotic gravitational perturbations, ejections, or collisions over billions of years, their orbits must remain spaced by several mutual Hill radii (typically at least 8 to 10 for long-term stability). In a star's habitable zone or total gravitational sphere of influence, this geometric and dynamical packing constraint limits the maximum number of stable coplanar planets.

Doesn't gravity attract objects in space until they collide?

Gravity pulls objects directly toward one another, but objects in orbit possess sufficient sideways (tangential) velocity. As gravity accelerates the object downward toward the central body, the object moves forward fast enough that the surface of the body curves away underneath it at the exact same rate. This continuous free fall without collision is what forms a stable closed orbit.
