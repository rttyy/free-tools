About this free tool

The Embedded Low-Power Battery Life & Duty Cycle Profiler is a dedicated tool for firmware developers and hardware engineers designing battery-operated sensors. It calculates realistic operating lifespan and evaluates instantaneous voltage sag across real battery chemistry curves. Use the free browser version to test your power budget, or purchase the standalone offline HTML version for air-gapped, zero-dependency engineering work.

How to use it

  1. Select your battery chemistry (CR2032, AA alkaline, 18650 Li-ion, or LiFePO4) and configure cell count.
  2. Enter your hardware's operating parameters, including MCU brownout reset voltage and sleep current in microamps.
  3. Add your active duty cycle states with execution time and current draw (e.g., sensor read, radio transmit).
  4. Analyze the predicted lifespan, peak voltage drop under load, and export your setup to JSON or CSV.

Frequently asked questions

How can I get my atmega328 to run for a year on batteries?

To achieve a year or more on batteries, use a bare ATmega328P running at 8MHz on its internal oscillator without an onboard USB bridge or power LED. Power it directly from 2x AA alkaline or 1x LiFePO4 cell to bypass inefficient linear regulators, put the chip into deep sleep (`SLEEP_MODE_PWR_DOWN`) with ADC and BOD disabled, and keep active tasks brief so your average current stays below 20-30 microamps.

Batteries. Why use 9V?

9V batteries (PP3) are usually a poor choice for modern low-power microcontrollers because they have very low capacity (around 400-500mAh) and contain six tiny 1.5V AAAA cells with relatively high internal resistance. If you step 9V down to 3.3V using a typical linear regulator, you waste more than 60% of the energy as heat; they are typically only used when compact high voltage is required for legacy sensors, op-amps, or smoke detectors.

Measure Lithium ion battery voltage (thus remaining capacity)

Because standard Li-ion cells maintain a flat discharge plateau between roughly 3.7V and 3.6V, measuring terminal voltage with an ADC only gives a rough estimate of state-of-charge and is heavily skewed under load. For accurate readings, sample the voltage using a high-value resistive divider only after the device has rested in sleep mode, or implement a dedicated I2C fuel gauge IC that uses Coulomb counting.

Smart and effective ways to light up a garage without relying on power outlets?

The most effective approach is pairing 12V LED strips with a 12V deep-cycle LiFePO4 or SLA battery recharged by a modest 20W-50W solar panel mounted outside with a charge controller. To minimize battery draw, place the lights on a low-quiescent-current 12V PIR motion sensor switch or a timed relay so illumination automatically cuts off when the garage is unoccupied.
