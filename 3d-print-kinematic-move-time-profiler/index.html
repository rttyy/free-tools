About this free tool

This free online tool simulates real Marlin jerk and Klipper junction deviation kinematics to accurately predict true 3D print completion times. Built for power users and print farm operators, it analyzes G-code to detect hotend volumetric flow bottlenecks before you press print. Upload your file to run a full motion analysis right in your web browser.

How to use it

  1. Upload your sliced G-code file or paste a G-code preview snippet.
  2. Select your firmware type (Marlin or Klipper) and input your machine acceleration, jerk, or junction deviation settings.
  3. Enter your hotend maximum volumetric flow rate limit (mm³/s).
  4. Review the simulated print time, motion profile graphs, and identified volumetric flow bottlenecks.

Frequently asked questions

Calculating the estimated print time of an already sliced file

To calculate the true print time of an already sliced file, upload your G-code to the profiler. Standard slicers only estimate time using target feedrates, but this tool parses the file line-by-line against your firmware's acceleration, jerk, and junction deviation rules to recalculate the actual toolhead travel time.

How to estimate the printing time of a 3D printer from an STL file?

An raw STL file contains 3D mesh geometry, not motion commands. To estimate printing time, you must first slice the STL into G-code based on your layer height, line width, and speed settings. Once sliced, pass the G-code through this kinematic profiler to simulate real-world motion control and get an accurate completion estimate.

Chitubox estimated time very away from the real time

Chitubox and resin/FDM slicers often miscalculate print times because they use fixed layer times or naive motor speeds without accounting for actual axis acceleration, peel delays, or firmware motion buffer limits. Running your sliced file through a dedicated firmware profiler accounts for physical motor constraints, giving you realistic completion times.

Why does my slicer print time estimate differ so much from real print time?

Slicers usually assume your printhead travels at full speed instantly. In reality, firmware caps speeds based on acceleration limits, jerk limits (Marlin), and cornering logic (Klipper junction deviation). On complex parts with short moves, your toolhead spends most of its time accelerating or decelerating, causing slicers to underestimate time by up to 50%.
