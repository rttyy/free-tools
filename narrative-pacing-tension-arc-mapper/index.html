About this free tool

The Interactive Story Pacing & Tension Arc Mapper is an open-access visualization tool designed for fiction writers, screenwriters, and narrative designers. It lets you plot chapter tension, identify narrative dead zones, and benchmark your progress against established narrative archetypes. Use it directly in your browser for free, or grab the offline single-file edition for disconnected writing sessions.

How to use it

  1. Add your story scenes or beats with brief summaries and assign a tension score from 1 to 10.
  2. Select an archetype overlay (3-Act, Save the Cat, Hero's Journey, or Freytag's Pyramid) to compare your narrative trajectory.
  3. Review the automated velocity heatmap and reader fatigue alerts to spot slump zones.
  4. Export your finished pacing chart and beat sheet as Markdown, CSV, JSON, or vector SVG.

Questions this tool answers

Where are my story's pacing slumps or reader fatigue zones, and how does my scene tension arc compare to a classic 3-Act Structure template?

You enter: List of scenes/chapters in order · Word counts per scene · Emotional valence rating for each beat (-5 heartbreak to +5 joy) · Conflict intensity score for each beat (1 to 10) · Narrative mystery level for each beat (0 to 10).
You get: A composite tension curve visualization mapped against target structure templates (e.g. 3-Act Structure), highlighting low-conflict slumps (>3 consecutive low-intensity scenes) and reader fatigue zones (>15,000 words without tension dip).

If I reorder my scenes or adjust word counts and tension scores, how will my overall reading duration, pacing density, and tension curve change?

You enter: Current scene sequence and parameter scores · Target reading speed (wpm) · Proposed scene reordering (e.g., move Scene 9 to Midpoint position).
You get: Recalculated manuscript reading duration in minutes/hours, revised pacing density (words per tension unit change), instant SVG tension curve refresh, and updated diagnostic warnings.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the free online version and the paid Gumroad product?

The free web version includes the complete pacing engine, archetype overlays, and export features. The $9 Gumroad purchase provides a standalone, single-file offline HTML package with lifetime updates, zero dependencies, and no need for an internet connection.

Where is my manuscript data stored?

All scene titles, tension ratings, and notes are processed entirely client-side in your local browser storage. No narrative data is ever sent to an external server or used for model training.

Can I compare two different drafts of the same story?

Yes. The Dual Draft Scenario Comparator allows you to load two pacing profiles side-by-side, making it easy to confirm whether developmental rewrites actually resolved flat spots in your narrative arc.

Which export formats are supported?

You can export your raw scene list and scores to Markdown, CSV, and JSON, or download publication-ready vector charts in SVG format for printing and visual reference binders.
