Frequently asked questions

How much fertilizer do I need for my vegetable garden?

Fertilizer quantity depends on your bed's square footage, the crop's nutrient demand, and the fertilizer's N-P-K percentage. On average, heavy vegetable feeders require roughly 0.15 to 0.20 pounds of actual elemental nitrogen per 100 square feet per season. Dividing that target weight by the decimal percentage of nitrogen on your fertilizer bag gives you the total weight of product needed.

How long does mulch last?

Organic mulches like wood chips or shredded bark typically last 1 to 3 years before fully decomposing into the soil. Finer organic materials like straw, grass clippings, or shredded leaves break down in 4 to 9 months and generally require replenishment every season. Inorganic mulches, such as black plastic or landscape fabric, last several seasons but do not improve soil organic matter.

How should vegetable seeds be sown evenly?

For tiny seeds like carrots and lettuce, mix the seed with fine sand or dry coffee grounds at a 1:3 ratio before sprinkling to spread them uniformly. You can also use a handheld dial seed sower, seed tape, or fold an envelope into a V-crease and tap it gently with your index finger while moving down the furrow. Thinning seedlings after emergence is still required for optimal spacing.

What causes fertilizer root burn and how do I prevent it?

Fertilizer burn occurs when high concentrations of soluble mineral salts accumulate in the root zone, drawing water out of plant roots through osmosis and causing tissue desiccation. To prevent it, calculate applications strictly by dry weight rather than volume, distribute granules evenly across the soil surface, water deeply after applying, and avoid applying high-nitrogen chemical salts to dry soil.
