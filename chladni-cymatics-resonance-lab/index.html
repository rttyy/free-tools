About this free tool

The Chladni Plate Cymatics & Resonance Sandbox is an interactive simulator designed for physics teachers, acoustics enthusiasts, sound designers, and generative artists. It models 2D modal vibrations and real-time particle physics without requiring physical sand or laboratory hardware. Use the free online version to visualize standing waves, listen to synchronized audio frequencies, and export geometric patterns.

How to use it

  1. Select mode numbers (m, n) or adjust frequency controls to set plate resonance.
  2. Toggle the Web Audio tone synthesizer to hear the exact pitch driving the modal pattern.
  3. Adjust particle count and surface friction to watch sand settle into nodal lines in real time.
  4. Export your modal pattern as a high-resolution PNG or download CSV frequency profiles.

Questions this tool answers

Given a plate geometry, dimensions, material, and mode numbers (m, n), what exact acoustic frequency excites the resonance, where will sand grains settle, and how does edge clamping alter the nodal pattern?

You enter: Plate geometry (square or circular) · Edge condition (free edge or clamped edge) · Plate width or diameter (e.g., 0.30 m) · Material sound speed / elastic plate constant (e.g., aluminum 5,100 m/s) · Modal order numbers m and n (integers 1 to 9).
You get: The calculated resonant frequency in Hz, the standing wave displacement field w(x,y), the theoretical nodal line geometry, and a live simulation of sand particles migrating away from high-acceleration antinodes into nodal zero-lines.

If I want to produce a specific target nodal count or circular ring pattern on a resonant membrane, what excitation mode (m, n) and driving amplitude will hold the grains steady without ejecting them off the plate?

You enter: Target pattern type (e.g., 3 concentric circular rings with no nodal diameters) · Plate radius (e.g., 0.15 m) · Particle damping / friction factor (e.g., 0.20) · Max allowable driving acceleration before grains escape plate perimeter.
You get: The exact Bessel harmonic modes (m=0, n=3), resonant frequency (Hz), safe driving amplitude threshold (0.0 to 1.0), and settling time for particles to reach equilibrium.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the free web page and the paid Gumroad product?

Can I use this simulator for physics classroom demonstrations?

Yes. It is built to demonstrate 2D standing waves, nodal lines, and harmonic modes clearly. You can export high-resolution images and CSV data profiles directly for student lab reports and physics assignments.

How does the particle simulator calculate sand movement?

The sandbox models plate displacement using standing wave equations for rectangular modal shapes. Particles experience forces proportional to local displacement amplitude gradients, driving them off anti-nodes toward zero-displacement nodal lines.

What system requirements are needed to run 25,000 particles at 60 FPS?

Any modern web browser supporting HTML5 canvas and Web Audio (such as Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge) on modern desktop or laptop hardware will run the simulation smoothly.
