About this free tool

The Camera FOV & Architectural Framing Planner is a spatial 2D simulator built for architectural photographers, videographers, and location scouts. It lets you visualize lens field of view, sensor crop overlays, and depth of field directly on room dimensions or floor plans before your shoot begins. Try the free web version in your browser or grab the offline desktop pack for set use.

How to use it

  1. Input your room dimensions or upload a 2D floor plan image into the canvas.
  2. Select your camera sensor size (Full Frame, APS-C, MFT) and target focal length.
  3. Position and rotate the camera icon to display real-time field-of-view cones, crop overlays, and hyperfocal points.
  4. Export your completed spatial diagrams as SVG files or PDF shot planning reports.

Questions this tool answers

Is there a mapping tool that will let me visualize a lens's angle of view?

Frequently asked questions

Why is 18% grey considered to be in the middle for photography?

Human visual perception of brightness is logarithmic rather than linear. An object reflecting 18% of available light is perceived by human eyes as visually halfway between pure black (0% reflection) and pure white (100% reflection). Because of this perceptual middle point, light meters and camera exposure systems use 18% neutral gray as their standard baseline.

Is there a mapping tool that will let me visualize a lens's angle of view?

Yes. The Camera FOV & Architectural Framing Planner lets you plot exact lens angles of view directly onto 2D spatial layouts or floor plans. You can adjust camera placement, focal lengths, and sensor sizes to visually see field-of-view boundaries before setting up on location.

With all other things equal, in a DSLR, will a larger sensor produce a sharper image?

Not automatically, because overall sharpness depends primarily on lens resolving power and optical quality. However, a larger sensor requires less magnification to display an image at a given target size, which reduces visible optical aberrations and provides superior signal-to-noise ratios, often resulting in higher perceived detail.
